I run a sari-sari store, so cigarettes are hard to ignore. People buy them by the stick, by the pack, and sometimes by the rim.

I started looking at the numbers because I was considering buying 20 rims.

The price I was given was 365 per rim.

Twenty rims would cost me 7,300.

If I sold each rim to another store for 395, I would make 30 per rim. If I sold all 20, that is 600 gross profit before transport, time, and any other cost.

At 400 per rim, the gross profit would be 35 each, or 700 for all 20.

That looked very different once I stopped looking at the selling price and looked at the actual margin.

In my own store, the economics are better because I can sell by pack or by stick. One rim has 200 sticks. At 2.50 per stick, one rim can produce 500 in sales. Against a cost of 365, that is 135 gross profit per rim if every stick sells.

But that is not the same business as wholesaling the rim. Retailing takes longer. The cash comes back slowly. And the inventory sits with me until customers buy it.

There was another issue too. Cigarettes can carry more risk than an ordinary fast-moving product when the source or compliance is unclear. I was not willing to take that risk for a margin of 30 or 35 per rim.

So I did not go ahead with the purchase.

The useful part was doing the calculation before buying.

A product can move fast and still be a bad deal. A cheap buying price can still leave almost nothing after the real costs. And gross profit is not the same as money I actually get to keep.

For a small store where every peso of working capital matters, that difference is enough to change the decision.
